# 4th Round — HR Interview (5–8 Years, Java Backend)

This file is **only for the HR / recruiter round**.

Rounds 1–2 were technical. Round 3 was the hiring manager. HR is checking **stability, honesty, money, joining date, and whether you will accept**.

They already believe you can code (or you would not be here). A bad HR round still kills the offer.

**What this round usually is (20–40 minutes)**

1. Short intro (non-technical)
2. Why you are leaving / why this company
3. Notice period, buyout, serving vs buying
4. Current CTC vs expected CTC (fixed, variable, bonus, stocks)
5. Location, WFO / hybrid, relocation, travel
6. Family / night shift / background check (light)
7. Offer process, documents, joining date
8. “Do you have questions?”

**How to use this file**

- All answers are **open**. Speak them out loud once.
- Fill every `[bracket]` with **true** numbers before the call. BGV (background verification) checks CTC, dates, and employer names.
- Keep answers short. HR is not your architect.
- Salary: know **Current CTC**, **take-home**, **fixed vs variable**, **expected CTC**, and your **walk-away number** before you join the call.
- Same story as Round 3 for “why leaving.” If HR and the manager hear two different stories, they will not trust you.

**Study time: about 1.5–2 hours**

| Time | What to finish |
|---|---|
| 0.3h | Intro + why leave + why us |
| 0.5h | CTC math + negotiation lines |
| 0.3h | NP, WFO, location, joining |
| 0.3h | Scenarios (counter-offer, two offers, gap) |
| 0.2h | Documents + questions to ask HR |

## 1. Opening

---

### Q. Tell me about yourself. (HR version)

**Answer (45–60 seconds)**

> I am a Java backend developer with **[X] years** of experience. I currently work on Nokia WS-NOC ESM — a service used to provision Ethernet services on the network. Before that I was at **[company]** for **[N] years**.
>
> I am looking for the next step in my backend career at **[this company]**. I can join after my notice period of **[60/90] days**.

**Stop.** Do not explain Kafka or HashMap. If they want tech, they will loop you back to engineering.

---

### Q. Walk me through your career in brief.

**Answer**

Latest first. Employer, duration, one line each.

> **[Current]** — **[start] to present** — Java backend on ESM.
>
> **[Previous]** — **[dates]** — Spring Boot services, **[domain]**.
>
> Total **[X] years**. No employment overlap. I can share offer letters and relieving letters as per BGV.

If you had a contract / vendor payroll: say it clearly. Hiding a third-party payroll is a common BGV fail.

---

### Q. Are you comfortable if I record this / take notes?

**Answer**

> Notes are fine. For recording, please confirm the purpose, who can access it, and the retention period. If that is part of the formal interview process and handled securely, I am comfortable.

## 2. Why leaving, why us

Use the **same** reason you gave the manager.

---

### Q. Why are you looking for a change?

**Answer (pick the true one, keep it clean)**

> I have completed **[N] years** on my current product and learned a lot. I want **[broader backend ownership / a domain I am interested in / a product-engineering culture / modern Spring Boot + cloud at greater depth]**. It is a planned move. I will serve my notice and do a proper handover.

**Acceptable extra (second sentence, not the first):** “Compensation is part of it; I want it to match the market for my experience. The work still matters.”

**Do not say**

- My manager is toxic (even if true — say “limited growth / process” instead)
- The company is sinking
- I am bored (sounds lazy)
- Only money, with no other reason
- I hate night support so I am running away (they have on-call too)

---

### Q. Why this company? Why this role?

**Answer**

One real fact about *them*:

> I applied because you work on **[product / domain / scale]**. The JD matches what I already do — Java, Spring, APIs, data, messaging — and I can add value from the first month. I also like **[their engineering / client / stability / product]** from what I have read.

If you cannot name one real fact, you look like you applied everywhere. Spend 10 minutes on their careers page before the call.

---

### Q. Why should we hire you? (HR version)

**Answer**

> You get **[X] years** of Java backend delivery, someone who has owned features through lab and production, and someone who will join on the date I commit. I am not job-hopping every year. I finish notice periods.

---

### Q. Why did you resign without an offer? (only if true)

**Answer**

> I resigned because **[true reason: health / family / personal / role ended]**. I understood the market risk. I used the time to **[prepare / family / upskill]**. I am interviewing with a clear target role, not applying to everything. I can join on **[date]** because I am already serving / served notice.

Do not invent “I wanted to travel the world” if you did not.

---

### Q. What did you not like in your current company?

**Answer**

> I would not say I dislike the company. I got good exposure on a real network product. The gap for me is **[growth in my next 3 years / tech stack direction / limited ownership]**. I am leaving on professional terms.

Never name a person.

---

### Q. What are you looking for in your next job?

**Answer**

> A Java backend role where I own services, work with a strong team, and can grow toward tech lead IC. Clear work, fair pay, and a joining date we both can keep.

## 3. Notice period and joining

This is one of the top reasons offers die. Be exact.

---

### Q. What is your notice period? Is it negotiable?

**Answer**

> My official NP is **[90] days** as per offer letter / HR policy. I will **try** to negotiate after I have a written offer, but I cannot promise a buyout today. I will not abscond. I can join on **[date = offer date + NP]**, earlier only if current HR approves.

If you already resigned:

> I have already resigned. Last working day is **[date]**. I can join from **[date + 1 or after garden leave]**. Relieving letter will be ready as per company process.

---

### Q. Can you buy out / get early relieving?

**Answer**

> Buyout is **[allowed / not allowed / case by case]** in my company. Recovery would be **[N days salary]** if that is the policy. I will check with HR **after** offer. I will not commit to 15-day joining if policy is 90 days — that would be a lie.

---

### Q. Will you serve full NP if we cannot wait?

**Answer**

> My official NP is 90 days and that is common in my current employment context. I will request early release after a written offer and keep you updated, but my reliable committed date is **[date]**. If the role has a hard earlier deadline, let us confirm whether buyout or phased onboarding is possible.

This sentence builds trust. Many HR people prefer a 90-day honest candidate over a 15-day story that slips.

---

### Q. What if your current company does not relieve you?

**Answer**

> I will follow the contract. I will keep you updated weekly during NP. I have no pending bond / **[I have a bond of X — disclose it]**. I will not join a second company on the same payroll dates.

---

### Q. Do you have a bond / service agreement?

**Answer**

> **No bond.**  
> or  
> **Yes — [N] months remaining, buyout [amount].** I disclosed this so there is no surprise in BGV.

Hidden bonds kill offers.

---

### Q. How soon can you join? Earliest joining date?

**Answer**

Give **one date**.

> Earliest realistic date: **[DD MMM YYYY]**. I prefer not to give a fake earlier date.

## 4. Salary — CTC, hike, negotiation

Do the math **before** the call. Use your last **increment letter + last 3 months payslips**.

Write this on paper:

| Item | Amount |
|---|---|
| Fixed CTC | ₹[ ] |
| Variable / bonus (target) | ₹[ ] |
| Actual variable last year | ₹[ ] |
| Stocks / RSU (annual vest, not full grant) | ₹[ ] |
| **Total current CTC you will quote** | ₹[ ] |
| Monthly in-hand (approx) | ₹[ ] |
| **Expected CTC** | ₹[ ] |
| **Walk-away** (below this you say no) | ₹[ ] |

In India, “CTC” often includes PF, gratuity, insurance, variable. Ask them **what is included** before you compare.

---

### Q. What is your current CTC?

**Answer**

> Current CTC is **₹[X] LPA**. Out of that, fixed is **₹[A]** and variable is **₹[B]**. Last year I received **₹[actual bonus]**. I can share payslips as per your process.

**Do not** quote only in-hand and call it CTC. **Do not** inflate. BGV and payslips will show the truth.

If you have RSUs: “Grant is ₹[G] over [N] years; I count **₹[G/N]** as annual, not the full grant.”

---

### Q. What is your expected CTC?

**Answer (calm, not a fight)**

> I am looking for **₹[Y] LPA**. That is about **[Z]%** over my current CTC and in line with **[X] years** in this market for a backend role. I am flexible in a small band if the rest of the offer is strong — role, location, joining date. I am not here only to maximise the number on day one.

How to pick Y:

- Check the company’s published band, comparable current offers, location, role scope, and the **fixed** component. Market percentages change; do not rely on one generic hike rule.
- Ask for a defendable number, not a random multiple of current pay.
- Do not say “as per company standards” only — they will lowball. Give a number, then flex.

---

### Q. What was your last hike and appraisal rating? What designation/band do you expect?

**Answer**

> My last revision was **[X]%** effective **[date]**. My rating was **[rating/description]** under my company’s scale. Current designation is **[title]**. For this role I care more about matching responsibilities and the company’s level framework than copying my current title, but I would like the offered level/band stated clearly.

Do not invent a rating; some employers verify increment letters or ask the former employer.

---

### Q. We can offer only ₹[lower]. Can you accept?

**Answer**

> Thank you. That is below what I expected. Can we look at **fixed** vs variable? If you can do **₹[middle]** fixed, I can close. I also care about **[joining bonus / NP buyout / relocation / extra leave]** if the CTC cannot move.

If it is still below walk-away:

> I appreciate the process. I cannot accept below **₹[walk-away]** — it would not be sustainable for me. I hope we can stay in touch if the band changes.

Never accept a number you will resent in month two, then stall on joining.

---

### Q. Why such a high hike?

**Answer**

> My current pay is **[below market / last hike was N months ago / variable is not guaranteed]**. The role here is **[wider / on-call / WFO city with higher cost]**. I am not doubling for no reason. I can show market data from the JD band if they published one.

---

### Q. What is your take-home? Can you share payslips?

**Answer**

> In-hand is about **₹[M]** per month after PF and tax. Yes, I can share the required payslips and Form 16 through the company/BGV vendor’s secure portal. I will follow their masking policy rather than emailing sensitive documents to an unverified personal address.

---

### Q. Is variable guaranteed?

**Answer**

> No. Target variable is **₹[B]**. Actual depends on company and rating. That is why I look at **fixed** closely.

When they say “CTC 25 LPA”, ask: “How much is guaranteed fixed?”

---

### Q. We have a 70-30 / 80-20 variable. OK?

**Answer**

> I prefer a higher fixed share because variable is not guaranteed. If the role is good I can accept a reasonable variable, but I need the **fixed** number in writing. I will not sign if most of the “hike” is only a bonus target.

---

### Q. Do you have ESOPs / joining bonus in mind?

**Answer**

> I am open to RSUs or a joining bonus if it helps close a CTC gap, especially if I lose unvested stock by leaving. I still need a fair monthly fixed. Please share vesting schedule — 4-year vest with 1-year cliff is common; I will not count unvested value as cash.

---

### Q. Current offer from another company — can you share?

**Answer**

> I have **[an offer / am in process]** at **₹[band]**. I am not using it as a threat. I am telling you so we can be efficient. I will decide on **role + people + money + date**, not only the highest PDF.

If you have no offer: “I do not have a signed offer yet. I am in other processes. I will not invent a fake offer letter.”

Fake competing offers are a known HR killer.

---

### Q. What is the lowest you can accept?

**Answer**

Do not give your walk-away first.

> I have shared my expectation of **₹[Y]**. I can stretch a little for the right team. Please share your band for this level — I will tell you clearly if we are in range.

## 5. Location, WFO, shifts, travel

Your sample interviews asked this. Answer once, truthfully.

---

### Q. Preferred location? Open to relocation?

**Answer**

> Preferred: **[city]**. Relocation: **[yes, I can move in N weeks / no, I need this city / hybrid only]**. If relocation: I will need **[time + relocation assistance if they have it]**. I will not say yes to Chennai if I will refuse later.

---

### Q. Current city, hometown, and commute you can sustain?

**Answer**

> I currently live in **[city/area]**; hometown is **[city]**. For this office I can sustainably commute **[time/distance]** for **[N] WFO days**. If the office location changes, I need that confirmed before signing.

---

### Q. Work from office / hybrid / remote?

**Answer**

> I can do **[5 days WFO / 3-2 hybrid / 2 days]**. Full remote: **[no / only if the JD says so]**. I will follow the team’s policy after joining. I will not accept a remote offer and then fight WFO in week one.

If WFO is a problem, say it **now**. They asked “any problem with WFO or location” in a real manager/HR loop.

---

### Q. Night shift / 24x7 support / US timings?

**Answer**

> I can do **on-call rotation** and occasional overlap with US/EU. I cannot do **permanent night shift** as a 5-day job **[if that is true]**. Please tell me the real roster for this team.

---

### Q. Are you open to travel / client site / onsite?

**Answer**

> Short travel: yes. Long onsite (months): **[yes with notice / no because family]**. Client-facing service-company deputation: **[ok / I am targeting a product role, please confirm this is not a bench-to-client move]**.

If you are joining a service company, ask: “Is this for a specific project or for the bench?”

---

### Q. Passport / visa? Ready for onsite?

**Answer**

> Passport: **[valid till date]**. Visa: **[none / H1 in process — never lie]**. I will not promise an onsite that needs a visa I do not have.

## 6. Stability, gaps, documents, BGV

---

### Q. Why so many job changes? (if it applies)

**Answer**

> Each move had a reason: **[project ended / better role / relocation]**. In my last role I stayed **[N] years**. I am looking for a place to stay 3+ years. I am not a 12-month hopper by plan.

If you have one short stint: “That one was a mismatch; I left professionally and I learned to ask better questions in HR rounds — which is what I am doing now.”

---

### Q. Explain this gap on your resume.

**Answer**

> From **[month]** to **[month]** I was **[serving NP / unemployed after layoff / family / health / study]**. I was not working for another company off the books. I can provide resignation/relieving records, PF history, Form 16, or other reasonable documents through the secure BGV process. Bank statements are highly sensitive and should be a last resort if the verifier specifically requires them.

---

### Q. Background verification — any issue?

**Answer**

> No. All employers, dates, and CTC I shared are accurate. I can provide:

- Resume (same dates as application)
- Offer letters + increment letters
- Last 3 months payslips
- Form 16 / tax
- Resignation / relieving / experience letters
- Education (degree, marksheets)
- PAN and address proof; Aadhaar only if the formal process requires it and through a secure portal
- Passport if needed

**Disclose before BGV**

- Different legal name (marriage)
- Degree still pending
- Worked via vendor / contractor
- Still serving NP (no relieving yet — normal)
- Any ongoing investigation (rare)

A surprise in BGV = offer revoked.

---

### Q. Highest education? Gap in studies?

**Answer**

> **[B.E. / B.Tech / MCA]** in **[year]** from **[college]**. No fake degree. If the college name on the JD must match UGC/AICTE, mine does / here is the story.

---

### Q. Are you related to anyone in this company?

**Answer**

> **No.**  
> or  
> **Yes — [name, team], we will not be in the same reporting line.** Hiding this is often a policy violation.

---

### Q. Any pending interviews with us in another requisition?

**Answer**

> I applied to **[this JD]**. If there is a duplicate profile, please merge. I am not running two offers in the same company without telling you.

## 7. Offer, counter-offer, multiple offers

---

### Q. If your current company gives a counter-offer, will you stay?

**Answer**

> I am not leaving only for a short-term raise. I would evaluate any counter-offer against the original reasons I started looking—role, growth, management, and stability—not panic money. I will not use your offer only as leverage. If I accept your written offer, my intention is to join.

HR asks this because many people take a counter and ghost. Say it like you mean it — and then actually mean it.

---

### Q. We will send an offer. How much time do you need to sign?

**Answer**

> I can review in **[24–48 hours / 2–3 days]**. I will not sit on it for two weeks. If I need a day to compare another process, I will say so. I will not sign and then renege.

---

### Q. Will you accept if we make an offer at expected CTC?

**Answer**

> If the written offer matches what we discussed — **role, location, CTC breakup, joining date** — I intend to accept. I need to see **fixed vs variable** and **WFO policy** in the letter. I will not give a fake “100% yes” before I read the PDF.

---

### Q. You have another offer releasing tomorrow. What will you do?

**Answer**

> I will ask them for the letter. I will tell you the deadline honestly so you can fast-track if you want me. I will not disappear. I will choose one and withdraw from the other professionally.

---

### Q. What if we delay the offer by two weeks?

**Answer**

> Please tell me the real timeline. I will not pause other processes for an undefined wait. If you are serious, even a verbal band + date helps.

## 8. Personal / HR screening

Keep personal answers boring and professional. You do not owe a life story.

---

### Q. Tell me about your family. Are you married? Planning kids?

**Answer**

> I stay with **[family / spouse / alone]**. I am **[married / single]**.

For potentially discriminatory or inappropriate questions (pregnancy planning, caste, religion as a hiring filter), keep it short: “I prefer to keep that personal. It will not affect my joining date or ability to do the role.” Exact legal treatment varies by jurisdiction; this guide is not legal advice.

---

### Q. Strengths and weaknesses? (HR)

**Answer**

Same as manager, shorter.

> Strength: I own what I ship and I do not hide delays. Weakness: I used to wait too long before asking for help; I now raise blockers the same day.

---

### Q. How do you handle pressure / deadlines?

**Answer**

> I cut scope with the lead, I do not silently drop tests. I have done release weeks. I do not live in 16-hour hero mode.

---

### Q. Are you a team player? Give an example.

**Answer**

> Yes. Example: I sat with QA on the first lab run of a southbound feature and wrote the retry steps for support. That is team play — not posters on the wall.

---

### Q. What are your hobbies? (they still ask)

**Answer**

One true hobby. Do not say “reading tech blogs” only.

> **[hobby]**. It is how I switch off so I do not burn out.

---

### Q. Where do you see yourself in 5 years? (HR)

**Answer**

> Growing as a senior Java backend engineer / tech lead IC. I want to stay and build here, not hop in 12 months.

---

### Q. Are you open to a service-company project / bench?

**Answer**

If they are TCS/Infosys/HCL-style:

> Please confirm the **project, location, and tech stack** in the offer or mail. I am joining for **[Java backend on X]**. I am not OK with an open-ended bench or a sudden stack change to something I did not interview for, without a discussion.

This is a 5–8 year answer. Juniors say yes to everything.

## 9. Scenario questions

---

### Q. We need you to join in 15 days or we drop the offer.

**Answer**

> I cannot honestly commit 15 days if NP is 90. I can request early release/buyout after a written offer and give you a weekly update. My reliable date remains **[date]**; I will not abscond or create overlapping employment.

---

### Q. Can you start as a contractor / consultant first?

**Answer**

> I prefer full-time employment. If it is a genuine contract-to-hire with a written conversion path, I will read the contract — rate, duration, who owns PF, termination. I will not start on a handshake.

---

### Q. Can you lower your expectation to enter, hike later?

**Answer**

> Promised future hikes are not a contract. I need a fair joining CTC. After that I will earn the next hike. I will not join 25% below market on a verbal “we will fix it in 6 months.”

---

### Q. You failed BGV on dates — what happened? (prep so it never happens)

**Answer**

Prevention: resume dates = offer letter dates = PF / Form 16. If a vendor payroll is in the middle, list **vendor and client** as your process requires.

If an honest mismatch (e.g. last working day vs last payroll day): “The relieving date is **[X]**; last paid day is **[Y]**. Here is the HR mail. It is not two jobs.”

---

### Q. Another company offered 4 LPA more. Will you drop us?

**Answer**

> If the difference is small and this role is better, I stay with you. If the difference is large and the work is equal, I have to be practical — I will tell you and give you a chance to match if you want. I will not accept you and then bounce in week two.

---

### Q. Will you sign a non-compete / long NP here?

**Answer**

> I will protect confidential information, intellectual property, and client data. I need to read any post-employment non-compete, non-solicit, geography, duration, and notice clause before signing. Enforceability varies by jurisdiction (and post-employment non-competes are treated narrowly in India), so I would get legal advice for an unusual restriction rather than make assumptions in the interview.

---

### Q. What is your probation period? What if the offer has different terms from the discussion?

**Answer**

> I will read the written offer before resigning. If role, location, fixed/variable split, notice period, probation, or joining date differs from our discussion, I will ask for a corrected letter or written clarification. Verbal promises do not override the contract.

---

### Q. Are you doing any freelancing or moonlighting?

**Answer**

> **[No.]** Or: **[I have a disclosed activity that does not compete or use employer time/IP.]** I will follow the employment policy and disclose anything that requires approval. I will not hide a second full-time job.

---

### Q. Can we contact your current manager for a reference now?

**Answer**

> Please contact my current employer only after a written offer / after I resign, because my search is confidential. I can provide former-manager or colleague references earlier if needed.

---

### Q. UAN/PF shows overlapping employment dates. How do you explain it?

**Answer**

> I will disclose any overlap before BGV and provide the documents. A short payroll overlap can happen because relieving date and final payroll posting differ; dual full-time employment is different. I will not alter the UAN/PF record or hide an employer. The resume, relieving letter, Form 16, and PF history should tell one consistent story.

---

### Q. Is your Full & Final settlement complete? Any recovery or assets pending?

**Answer**

> **[Completed / expected by date].** I have returned **[laptop/ID/assets]** and any recovery is **[none / amount and reason]**. Relieving and experience letters are **[available / issued after F&F]**. I will disclose a pending recovery rather than let it surprise BGV.

---

### Q. Are you affected by a layoff? Why were you selected?

**Answer**

> Yes, my role was affected as part of **[team/product/location]** reduction, not a misconduct exit. I can share the separation letter. Here is what I delivered and the feedback I received. I am available from **[date]**.

Do not sound ashamed. A layoff is not a performance confession.

---

### Q. Do you need visa sponsorship or have work-authorization limits?

**Answer**

> My current work authorization is **[status]**, valid until **[date]**. I **[do/do not]** need sponsorship. I will provide documents to the immigration team; I will not guess on legal eligibility.

---

### Q. Any planned leave immediately after joining?

**Answer**

> I have **[no planned leave / leave on dates X–Y]**. I am disclosing it before the joining date so the team can plan. I will not hide a booked two-week trip until day one.

---

## 10. Questions you should ask HR

Ask **2 or 3**. You look prepared, not desperate.

**Must ask before you sign**

1. Can I get the **CTC breakup** — fixed, variable, PF, gratuity, bonus target, joining bonus?
2. What is the **WFO / hybrid** policy for *this* team, not the company slogan?
3. What is the **joining date** you need, and is NP **[90]** acceptable?
4. What is the **BGV** list and timeline? Does the offer depend on BGV before or after joining?
5. Is this role **confirmed on a project** (service companies) or a product team?

**Also useful**

6. Appraisal cycle — when is the first review?
7. Relocation / notice-period buyout support?
8. Health insurance coverage (parents or not)?
9. Next steps and who sends the letter?
10. What are probation, termination/notice terms, joining-bonus clawback, and first appraisal eligibility?
11. Are role title, level, reporting location, and project/team named in the written offer?

**Avoid as your only questions**

- “How much leave in the first month?”
- “Can I WFH forever?”
- Asking CTC every two minutes after they already told you the band

---

## 11. What you must never do

- Lie about CTC, last working day, or degree
- Fake an offer letter from another company
- Give two different “why leaving” stories to HR and manager
- Accept WFO / city / NP you will refuse later
- Abscond or skip handover
- Sign an offer and keep shopping with no intent to join (reneging hurts you in the same network)
- Argue rudely about ₹50,000 when the gap is role fit
- Share current company’s confidential data as “I can bring clients / source code”
- Bad-mouth Nokia / TCS / any employer by name

## 12. Simple negotiation script (use this)

**When they ask expectation**

> Current CTC is **₹[X]** (fixed **₹[A]** + variable **₹[B]**). I am targeting **₹[Y]**. Please share your band for this level.

**When they come ₹[low]**

> Thanks. If we can close at **₹[middle]** on **fixed**, I can accept. If CTC cannot move, can we add **[joining bonus / NP buyout / relocation]**?

**When it is good enough**

> This works for me subject to the **written breakup and joining date**. Please send the letter. I will respond in **[48 hours]**.

**When it is not**

> I don’t think we are aligned on compensation. Thank you for the process. I will stay in touch if the band opens.

Say it without anger. HR talks to the hiring manager; you want them as an ally.

## Night-before checklist (Round 4)

1. Current CTC = fixed + real variable. Payslips ready.
2. Expected CTC and walk-away written on paper.
3. NP in days + earliest **calendar date**.
4. WFO / city / shift — the true constraint.
5. Why leaving = same as Round 3, one minute.
6. Why this company = one fact from their site.
7. Bond / vendor payroll / gap — disclosed if any.
8. No fake competing offer.
9. Three questions: breakup, WFO, BGV/joining.
10. Documents folder: resume, offers, payslips, Form 16, ID, education.

## How to talk in Round 4

1. Be polite and specific. HR remembers difficult people.
2. Numbers first, then flexibility. Not the other way round.
3. If you do not know a policy (buyout), say “I will confirm with my HR after offer.”
4. Smile when you say no. A calm no is still a no.
5. After the call, send a short thank-you mail with **expected CTC, NP, and location** in one paragraph so there is a paper trail.
